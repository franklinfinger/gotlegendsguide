# Product Experience and UI Standard

## North star

The game manages the player's collection. GOT Legends Guide teaches the player how to use that collection intelligently.

Every proposed feature should answer: would an Old Peeps on Porches teammate open this while actively playing GOT: Legends because it improves a decision? Favor practical battle strategy over complicated features.

The app should help players understand combinations, reasons, counters, vulnerabilities, target priority, substitutions, mechanics, factions, traits, skills, statuses, Raid, Alliance War, dragons, and team construction. Keep strategic depth beneath a simple experience.

## Intent-first experience

The eventual product is a GOT: Legends Strategy Command Center. Likely intents include winning a Raid, preparing for War, fighting a Dragon, looking up a Champion, countering an opponent, building better teams, and asking a game question. These are decision cues, not fixed navigation labels.

Design toward useful answers in seconds. Explain why a recommendation works whenever practical. Prefer flexible cores, complementary champions, substitutions, and strategic principles over rigid five-champion prescriptions.

## Mobile and visual quality

Mobile is the primary use case: players may consult the guide on an iPhone during a battle. Evaluate tap targets, thumb reach, density, navigation, search, scrolling, text legibility, portrait treatment, horizontal overflow, loading behavior, and speed to battle information. Desktop must remain strong.

The visual language should be purpose-built, compact, highly readable, strategy-focused, and game-adjacent without copying the game UI. Information hierarchy matters more than decoration.

Avoid generic AI/SaaS conventions: gratuitous gradients, glassmorphism, oversized heroes, large empty areas, endless rounded-card grids, generic purple/blue palettes, decorative widgets, excessive pills, unnecessary motion, or complexity used as decoration.

## Design process

Do not redesign everything in one pass. For a major UI effort:

1. Define the design language: typography, spacing, navigation, controls, cards, champion/status presentation, and interaction patterns.
2. When requested, prepare a small number of intentional visual directions for review.
3. Apply an approved direction to one representative screen, normally the home experience.
4. Obtain approval before expanding that language across the app.

Use supplied visual references when available. Keep a consistent visual language across screens; do not independently invent one for each page.
